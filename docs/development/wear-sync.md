# Phone/Wear sync and offline alarms

Read `wear/build.gradle`, phone `WearSync`/`WearPhoneListener`, watch `PhoneSync`, `PhoneFallback`, `PhoneMirror`, `PhoneDismissalJob`, `WearWatchListener`, and shared `AlarmStore` before changing these flows. See [README Wear OS companion](../../README.md#wear-os-companion) for user-visible limits. Current behavior is version-specific; inspect both peers when changing a protocol.

## Ownership and snapshot acceptance

The watch has its own alarms. The phone owns synchronized phone alarms and groups; phone create/edit/delete/toggle mutations require phone confirmation and must never silently become watch-local alarms. Snoozing an already ringing phone occurrence has the explicit, visibly watch-only safety branch described below; it does not transfer the recurring phone schedule. Data Layer requires compatible same-package/same-signer companions and a reachable peer. A sent message is not a receipt. Reassemble only bounded, complete snapshots; fence by owner node, publisher epoch, revision and publication ordering. Snapshot and acknowledgement can arrive in either order; UI confirmation requires the acknowledged owner/epoch and a snapshot at or beyond the committed revision. Do not hold store/identity locks across network waits. Reconnect and repair jobs must propagate persistence/acceptance failures and account for work arriving while a job runs.

For phone create/edit/delete, persist an uncertainty barrier before dispatch, bind receipts to the intended node/request/epoch and commit the receipt with the phone mutation. Do not automatically retry an uncertain create. A missing receipt is not proof of absence; bind negative status replies to their lookup attempt while accepting valid late positive receipts. Check the phone before explicit abandonment, warn about duplicates, and keep bounded non-evicting receipt capacity visible. Revision-checked edits may have committed even if the snapshot has not arrived. These guarantees belong to the companion protocol and do not give the external Binder API an idempotency key.

## Independent fallback is not mirroring

A cached list or a phone-ring mirror alone cannot alert when the phone dies before firing. The watch schedules separate device-protected fallback generations from the last accepted snapshot; offline edits/dismissals can leave them stale. Preserve the phone's first published occurrence instant across device zones and import both already-active one-shots and recurring occurrences without losing later recurrence. Do not suppress a watch trigger solely because the phone was recently reachable. Surface denied access, scheduling, capacity and pending-retry errors. The current fallback cap is 350 phone alarms; check source if changing limits.

`AlarmStore.syncPhoneFallback` validates the whole desired snapshot, persists it **with its owner** and a recovery wakeup before staging any registrations, and journals in-flight/obsolete generations. Retain the old working generation on replacement failure; commit before canceling obsolete PendingIntents. Recovery cleans staged tokens and retries desired state without the phone, even when an old token exists. Reject older revisions and retired publisher epochs, including when a desired replacement has not yet been applied. Bound old plus staged registrations and preserve native watch alarms.

Protect due and ringing occurrences during replacement or owner/epoch change. Future phone-imported snoozes are phone-owned and are canceled on accepted deletion/disable, rather than protected merely because `_snoozeAt` exists. Retiring a ringing recurring phone row also retires its future token. Marked legacy watch-local snoozes migrate to independent visible one-shots, retaining their pending generation; ambiguous legacy provenance remains protected and visibly uncertain. Track active-token replay tombstones for both recurring and one-shot imports, including active occurrences observed while a local occurrence is protected; a dismissed recurring occurrence must not reappear, but its future recurrence must survive. Phone-to-watch occurrence-specific closure requires verified shared identity, not merely alarm ID, approximate time or connectivity. The phone snapshot includes up to eight committed snooze lineage transitions per alarm, linking the original occurrence and session to the committed snooze generation and time. A matching accepted transition can replace a protected fallback with the phone's snooze; validate the full chain and current authoritative state, retain old state on registration failure, and preserve local-snooze conflicts. Older peers, missing history or ambiguous identities remain protected and may leave the desired state pending. Histories and registrations are bounded; inspect failure paths rather than evicting safety records silently.

## Snooze coordinator and receipts

`WatchSnooze` owns every watch snooze entry point: `AlarmActivity`, `PhoneMirrorActivity` and shared `AlarmReceiver` notification actions via the role-specific `WearSync.routeSnooze`. Watch-owned alarms call only the local engine. Phone-derived/mirrored sessions require a unique accepted owner/epoch/phone ID/shared occurrence join to select the exact phone session token. Never guess from ID/time or reachability. Mirror templates take the saved alarm's outputs, not its temporary sound-muted presentation. The phone validates its current exact session and snooze allowance again.

`SnoozeProtocol` uses `/openclock/v2/snooze`, `/openclock/v2/snooze-status` and `/openclock/v2/snooze-reply`, independent of the legacy generic command reply. Requests bind version 2, unique request ID, phone owner node, publisher epoch, alarm ID, exact phone session token, occurrence ID, selected minutes and issued time. The phone verifies the target node and epoch and commits the snooze plus durable receipt atomically in `AlarmStore`. The receipt repeats the binding and includes resulting occurrence ID, snooze time, used count and revision. Writes have a 15-second admission/commit window with five-second future-clock tolerance. Status lookups are read-only and can return old receipts without admitting a write. Reusing an ID with different binding fails. Receipt capacity is 128. New phone snooze commits reclaim receipts older than 48 hours, or the oldest admission-expired receipt under capacity pressure; the issued-time replay floor is persisted atomically with eviction and the alarm mutation, preventing re-admission after clock rollback. Unexpired write admissions are never evicted. A compacted receipt may return unknown on status lookup, never proof of non-commit. Pre-commit capacity/rejection is distinct from unknown outcome. Neither successful transport nor a legacy reply establishes commit.

Before dispatch, `beginWatchSnooze` commits a bounded `_watchSnoozes` intent and arms shared recovery. Transport runs off the UI thread, bounded by discovery/send timeouts and a five-second reply wait, with an independent ten-second local decision timer so queued transports cannot indefinitely postpone branching, and with no store/UI lock held. OS suspension and store I/O can still delay that timer. The caller rechecks its source before sending. A positive receipt installs the resulting phone-owned fallback before closing the matching watch source. The accepted snapshot and newer publisher/revision state fence late effects; an old receipt cannot restore a deleted/edited phone schedule or cancel a newer active watch session. Snapshot-before-receipt and receipt-before-snapshot use the same committed generation/lineage rather than recreating the original ring. Registration failure retains the original and durably records the receipt for retry. A receipt-before-fire replacement removes the source imported ring's active deadline/token/first-fire metadata: the committed snooze is a new occurrence with its own normal ring timeout, not a continuation of the old session.

Offline, rejected, missing exact identity, unsupported protocol or unknown outcomes branch locally: register a fresh watch-owned one-shot, preserve outputs/label/selected interval/remaining allowance, commit it with correlation and source retirement, then clear only matching controls. Keep the phone's future recurrence separate, including durable retry when a due mirror fallback is retired before firing. Branch time is request-relative, so restart does not restart the snooze duration. Natural ring expiry does not discard the intent; explicit dismissals/atomic mirror tombstones prevent resurrection. The normal ten-minute catch-up bound still applies and missed snoozes are reported. Do not enqueue an offline snooze write for replay.

Recovery uses only status reads (rate-limited per process), never another mutation. It runs from shared recovery/startup, local commits and phone refresh/reconnect signals. A confirmed late receipt is retained, but **never automatically deletes an independent branch**: phone and watch may both ring. Pending intents, unapplied receipts, watch-only alarms and unknown duplicate risk remain visible in the list/complication. OS scheduling, permissions, background execution and first-unlock restrictions still limit recovery; no deadline is guaranteed.

New peers advertise `snoozeProtocol: 2` and active snooze settings/counts. Old peers are never treated as supporting durable receipts. For old mirrors/imported active occurrences, exact published lineage or an exact committed fallback may establish the count; otherwise snooze is refused with the original intact, because the remaining allowance cannot safely be invented. Exact active used-count metadata on new peers resolves that uncertainty without matching by time. Update both companions for the complete path. Clearly marked old `_phoneLocalSnooze` generations migrate in place to visible one-shot watch ownership. Unknown provenance is not guessed. Both histories are capped at 128, but resolved history is reclaimed rather than permanently consuming capacity. Watch compaction at new intent admission retains source suppression for at least eleven minutes and while the exact local source session exists, preserves a single per-fallback receipt proof needed by lineage, and persists an expired-mirror replay floor. Pending intents/unapplied receipts are never age-evicted. Unknown branch lookup is attempted for 48 hours; correlation survives longer while the independent branch has a live or retry generation. Expiry never cancels that branch or proves no phone commit. Only settled, expired unknown branches can be reclaimed. Genuine unresolved/in-flight capacity failure still preserves the original ring. Subsequent independent watch snoozes do not consume these histories.

## Dismissal and other presentation

The current explicit watch-dismiss behavior is intentionally broader than occurrence matching: it requests dismissal of **all currently ringing** phone alarms, including unrelated ones. Snooze and automatic expiry do not send this generic request. The watch uses one request ID for the initial send and at most three retries aimed two seconds apart, stopping on valid acknowledgement; the request expires after 20 seconds. The phone atomically records the receipt with dismissal, including when nothing rings, so a replay cannot dismiss a later occurrence. Do not cancel future/snoozed alarms; a delayed first request can still affect alarms active when it arrives. Receipt capacity, clock skew and OS background limits matter. Local silence is not phone confirmation; outbox saturation must not block local dismissal. Trace local, fallback, mirror, notification, activity and gesture paths and guard cleanup against new sessions.

The watch complication reads committed native/independent watch triggers and committed phone fallback registrations joined to accepted phone metadata through `WatchSchedule`, not an independently calculated schedule. A missing/disabled phone row cannot hide a retained due/ringing fallback; retired/unmatched rows remain uncertain candidates. Phone rows without committed fallback do not contribute a claimed watch trigger. List and complication selection share this join, while phone editing controls still target the phone's accepted identity. Unavailable, stale, unconfirmed, erroneous or pre-unlock state is unknown, not empty; keep uncertainty and timeline expiration conservative and avoid a provider/sync feedback loop. Watch faces may ignore timelines or truncate text. The experimental double-shake heuristic is app-local, not proof of an OEM gesture; scope listeners to ringing services, revalidate the admission anchor, then dismiss the live watch sessions and mirrors at one cutoff and queue one generic phone request. Future and snoozed generations remain scheduled. Neither screen wake nor background Activity launch is guaranteed on Wear OS; the watch list provides a Ringing now route to the exact active controls.

## Complication refresh

For refresh changes, inspect `wear/src/main/java/com/openclock/ComplicationUpdateJob.java`, `NextAlarmComplication.java`, `ComplicationPolicy.java`, the watch manifest, and the relevant `WearSync`, `PhoneSync` and `WearWatchListener` call sites. `WearSync.afterCommit` signals watch-local commits; phone sync and listener events also signal changes, but a phone edit is not watch state until the corresponding snapshot has been delivered and accepted. `ComplicationClockReceiver` forces refreshes for the boot, package, clock/time-zone and exact-alarm-permission events declared in the manifest. The provider reads committed state; a provider request must not start another phone sync or publication loop.

`ComplicationUpdateJob.changed` persists a versioned dirty/force marker in device-protected storage. A queued direct worker waits 500 ms to coalesce nearby notifications, compares a local/phone fingerprint and requests `requestUpdateAll()` for changed, forced or unreadable state; it checks the version again before clearing the marker so an intervening event is not lost. The previous two-credit/five-minute allowance has been removed. Repeated changes may request more often than [Android's recommended five-minute average](https://developer.android.com/training/wearables/complications/exposing-data); preserve that tradeoff explicitly rather than claiming the system guarantees immediate refresh.

The job requests a persisted recovery job with a ten-second override deadline, and the manifest separately requests a 900-second periodic system refresh. Both are best effort: scheduling, process death, background restrictions, failed persistence or requests, connectivity, ambient mode and the watch face can delay or prevent a visible update. A failed drain can leave the marker dirty; a later event or the periodic provider request may help, but no particular recovery deadline is guaranteed. Preserve forced refresh, version fencing and conservative unknown-state handling when changing this path. Check events arriving during draining or job completion, failure paths and provider/sync loops; successful requests and builds do not prove on-device rendering or timing.

## Verification matrix

Exercise production code with disposable fakes: snapshot-before-ack and ack-before-snapshot; node/epoch replacement, stale revisions and replay; initial import while a one-shot or recurrence is active; due/ringing/snoozed preservation; local dismiss and recurrence tombstones; capacity, registration and durable-write failures. Inject death before/after desired-state commit, staged-journal writes, OS registration, replacement commit and obsolete cancellation; restart in a fresh process and verify exact owner, recovery wakeup, cleanup, retry without phone, and old-generation protection. Test duplicate/lost/delayed dismiss acknowledgements, no-active receipt, concurrent new sessions, outbox limits and expiry. Exercise complication uncertainty and notification/gesture routes separately.

A successful host probe or APK build does not verify physical offline delivery, reboot recovery, sound, vibration, watch wake, gestures, watch-face rendering or cross-device reliability. Device tests require explicit authorization; keep a trusted backup until verified.
